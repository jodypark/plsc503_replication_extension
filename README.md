# plsc503_replication_extension_fazekas2023

This repository contains the code and data for the replication and extension of Acs (2025) and Fazekas et al. (2023) for the final project of PLSC 503 (Instructor: Professor Bruce A. Desmarais). 

## Abstract

Federal contracting, worth more than \$700 billion annually, is systematically shaped by political donations. However, the donation-to-favoritism relationship varies substantially across agencies. This paper argues that agency ideology is a key moderator of this relationship. I test this argument by extending Fazekas et al.'s (2023) agency independence model with Acs's (2025) dynamic agency ideal point estimates. The replication reproduces the finding that the donation effect on the favoritism risk index is 0.131 points larger in cabinet agencies than in independent agencies ($p = 0.006$), with no significant baseline effect at the 5 percent level in independent agencies. The extension finds that regulatory agencies exhibit a larger donation-favoritism relationship, especially for medium donations to the party in power and in the DDD specification around the 2009 administration change. The continuous president-agency distance measure provides mixed evidence: firms are more likely to donate as agencies become more ideologically distant from the president, which cuts against the simple proximity version of the amplification hypothesis. These findings most clearly support a discretion mechanism and suggest that recent executive branch politicization may expand the scope of donation-linked favoritism in federal contracting.
