
## Memo (will be deleted)

- overview of the research: 
  - Agency ideology shapes the degree of favoritism toward politically connected firms, with more conservative agencies exhibiting stronger favoritism toward firms donating to the Republican Party
  - Together, these findings suggest that the politics of
federal contracting is shaped not just by formal institutional structure (i.e., the agency independence-tier
classification), but by the continuous ideological alignment between agencies and the political executive

- hypotheses
  - H1: (e.g., more conservative agencies show stronger favoritism toward Republican donors)

- equations
   - The key parameter of interest is $\beta_2$. Under the *amplification hypothesis*, $\beta_2 > 0$ (larger ideological distance reduces the donation-favoritism effect; smaller distance amplifies it)
   - Under the *insulation hypothesis*, $\beta_2 < 0$ or the relationship is non-monotonic.
   - A negative $\hat{\beta}$ indicates that firms donate more when the agency's ideology is close to the president, consistent with strategic targeting of politically aligned agencies.

- OIRA: Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs
- Mechanism: \begingroup\scriptsize Not just by formal institutional structure, but by continuous, administration-specific ideological alignment between agencies and the political executive\endgroup
- \begingroup\scriptsize Donations $\times$ agency moderator $\rightarrow$ Favored contracting\endgroup
